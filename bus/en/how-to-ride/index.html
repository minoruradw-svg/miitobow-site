How to ride a bus in Japan (general guide)

Boarding and payment differ by operator and route. Follow the signs on the bus and at the stop, and the driver’s instructions. This is only a general outline; fares and times are not included.

  • Boarding: Some buses are boarded at the front door, others at the rear (middle) door. Signs on the bus or at the stop show which.
  • Numbered ticket / IC card: On rear-boarding buses, take a numbered ticket (seiriken) or tap your IC card on the reader when you get on. The ticket number sets your fare. On many routes you also tap your IC card when you get off (follow the on-board instructions).
  • Fares: Some routes have a flat fare; others charge by distance. A display above the driver shows the fare for each ticket number.
  • Paying: Cash goes into the fare box when you get off (or when you get on, on pre-pay routes). Many buses do not give change, so use the change machine first. Transit IC cards (Suica, ICOCA, nimoca, etc.) are accepted by many operators, but not on every route or in every area.
  • Getting off: Listen for the next-stop announcement or display, then press a stop button. Stay seated until the bus stops.
  • Same stop name: Stops with the same name may be across the road or some distance apart depending on direction. This map groups same-name stops together.

For details, see “How to ride” on each operator’s official site (open it from “Official sites” on a stop or route screen).

• “How to ride” is a general outline, not guidance for a specific operator or route.
